\subsection{Representative Predicate Decisions}
The following task--predicate pairs show distinct failure modes. Predicate text and saved Boolean decisions are reproduced directly; the medical task is an explicitly shortened excerpt.

\paragraph{False-negative judgment (nonrequired skill accepted).}
For the BM25 TheoremQA task ``Please solve \texttt{x\textasciicircum{}3 + 2*x = 10} using newton-raphson method,'' the business-model skill's predicate is: ``Generate Business Model Canvas 9-block analysis, revenue modeling, unit economics, and cost breakdown for a validated business concept.'' The saved decision is \texttt{True}. The requested numerical root-finding task does not ask for any of the named business deliverables, illustrating a predicate-judgment error rather than a broadly worded mathematical predicate.

\paragraph{False-positive judgment (required skill rejected).}
The Vector task asks: ``Calculate the de Broglie Wavelength of a tennis ball of mass 57 g traveling 25 m/s in meters.'' Its required wave-theorems skill has the predicate: ``These problems require finding a wavelength using the relationship between wave speed and frequency, or using Bragg's law for diffraction angle, or using the de Broglie relation for particle momentum.'' The saved decision is \texttt{False}, although the de Broglie alternative explicitly covers the requested operation.

\paragraph{Restrictive predicate.}
The SkillSight task asks: ``Company A is currently trading at \$150 per share, and earnings per share are calculated as \$10. What is the P/E ratio?'' The required earnings-multiplier skill has the predicate: ``task requires computing price to earnings multiples or growth-to value ratios from dividend discount model inputs including required rate of return, growth rates, and payout ratios.'' The saved decision is \texttt{False}. The extra dividend-discount qualifications exclude a direct price-to-earnings calculation, showing a restriction in the predicate itself rather than establishing that every false positive has this cause.

\paragraph{Medical-calculator judgment.}
A MedCalc-Bench task includes the instruction excerpt: ``Using the Calcium Correction for Hypoalbuminemia formula, what is the patient's corrected calcium in mg/dL?'' The accompanying note reports calcium of 7.2 mg/dL and albumin of 3.2 g/dL. SkillSight retrieves the annotated calcium-correction skill, whose predicate reads: ``The task determines patients' corrected calcium levels by adjusting total serum calcium values for hypoalbuminemia using a standardized albumin correction calculation.'' The saved decision is \texttt{False}. Here the requested calculation is explicitly named in both task and predicate; narrowing alone does not explain the mismatch.
