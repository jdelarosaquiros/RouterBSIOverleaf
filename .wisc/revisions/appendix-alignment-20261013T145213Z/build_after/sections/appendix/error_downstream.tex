\subsection{Routing Errors Within Downstream Disagreements}
We match downstream outcomes by task and retriever, then restrict to cases where $\mathrm{RouterBSI}_{\mathrm{top}5}$ answers incorrectly and SkillRouter answers correctly. This gives 21 task--retriever cases in each dataset: 16 distinct TheoremQA tasks and 21 distinct MedCalc-Bench tasks. Table~\ref{tab:app-conditional-errors} distinguishes the five supplied skills from the underlying predicate-selected set.

For the actual supplied top-five sets, TheoremQA has 89 FN and 5 FP occurrences, while MedCalc-Bench has 90 FN and 6 FP occurrences. The corresponding FN shares of FP+FN are 94.68\% and 93.75\%, respectively. These extra-selection shares are not rates of required-skill predicate rejection or proportions of tasks with incorrect predicate judgments. Top-five padding can supply a nonrequired skill even when its predicate is false, so a top-five FN need not be a predicate FN. Since every task has one annotated required skill and receives five skills, even a set containing the required skill has four non-gold entries.

The required skill is present in 16 of the 21 TheoremQA cases and 15 of the 21 MedCalc-Bench cases. All six MedCalc-Bench top-five misses occur with BM25, despite the required skill being present in its original candidate pool. On the same downstream subsets, the predicate-selected sets contain 24 FN and 12 FP occurrences for TheoremQA, but 0 FN and 8 FP occurrences for MedCalc-Bench. SkillRouter succeeds on these matched cases with 88 non-gold supplied occurrences for TheoremQA and 84 for MedCalc-Bench. Thus, missing required skills cannot explain all failures, and the presence of non-gold context alone does not demonstrate that it caused an incorrect answer.
