Stronger backbones improve initialization, while optimization brings smaller model Qwen3.5 4B close to the optimized performance of GPT-OSS 120B. 8 Conclusion We presented RouterBSI, a non-parametric framework that refines explicit skill applicability predicates from routing failures without updating model weights. It combines minimum Bayes risk selection over bounded replay with held-out validation. Experiments on six SRA-Bench datasets show that RouterBSI achieves the strongest average routing performance among evaluated methods and preserves more required skills as the selection budget decreases. Ablations support the complementary contributions of replay and validation and show improvements across model backbones. These findings demonstrate the value of editable predicates for feedback-driven skill routing, although stronger routing does not consistently yield the best downstream performance. Future work can incorporate skill dependencies to better connect skill selection with downstream task requirements. Limitations Our approach has three limitations. First, training is computationally expensive, although optimization converges on the training data and improves overall routing performance. Second, we do not provide a protocol for determining which skills a task requires. The method therefore relies on human annotations or an external labeling mechanism, making optimization dependent on the availability and accuracy of these labels. Third, we keep the predicate checker’s instructions fixed, even though errors in interpreting predicates can lead to incorrect skill selections. Our algorithm indirectly improves checking performance by generating and selecting predicate formulations that the checker evaluates more accurately against labeled tasks; however, it does not directly address weaknesses in the checker’s instructions. Ethics Statement Our experiments reuse existing SRA-Bench tasks, annotations, and skill content, which may contain errors, biases, or harmful instructions. Reuse should respect dataset, skill, and model licenses, while sensitive task data require appropriate access controls and retention policies, particularly when using external model services. Incorrect predicate judgments can omit required skills or admit inappropriate ones, propagating errors to downstream decisions. Bounded replay and held-out validation assess routing behavior rather than factual correctness, fairness, or safety. Results on MedCalc-Bench do not establish clinical validity or suitability for patient care; high-stakes applications require domain-specific validation and qualified human oversight. Retrieved instructions and generated code should be treated as untrusted content, with permission controls and isolated execution before use.
