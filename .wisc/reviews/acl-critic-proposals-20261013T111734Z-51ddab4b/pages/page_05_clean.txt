We evaluate skill routing and downstream performance conditioned on the selected skills. For skill routing, we report Recall@k and nDCG@k. We additionally report F1@k to compare filtering capabilities. We also report retention as Ret@k to compare the percentage of required skills retained in top k from the retrieved skills. For downstream performance, each benchmark follows its standard evaluation protocol to measure success rate: rulebased exact match or accuracy for reasoning and QA datasets, and pass@1 based on unit-test execution for BigCodeBench. Averages are measured across task instances. 5.3 Selected Models. We evaluate five models spanning four open-weight checkpoints and one proprietary frontier model: Qwen3.5-4B (Qwen Team, 2026a), Qwen3.8-27B (Qwen Team, 2026b), Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (Meta, 2024), gpt-oss-120b (OpenAI, 2025), and GPT-5.4 (OpenAI, 2026). 5.4 Baselines We study retrieval, reranker, and selection baselines. The retrievers include sparse and dense retrievers (Su et al., 2026) and SkillSight, a hybrid retriever. They are trained on training split and frozen on validation and test splits. LLM Select is a static router, and LLM Direct and LLM Oracle are downstream performance baselines representing without skills and with ground-truth skill set, respectively (Su et al., 2026). The non-parametric optimization baselines are SkillDag and Skill-Pro (Mi et al., 2026; Bai et al., 2026). RouterBSIinit and RouterBSI refer to the cold-start and final iteration performance. RouterBSIstrict selects skills only when their predicate evaluates to true. RouterBSItopk are the top k skills using the sum of their predicate label and the retrieval score. 5.5 Implementation Details. The retrievers use either pretrained embedding models and/or lexical matching, so they do not require training. Candidate skills are retrieved from the shared external skill corpus in response to the input query. The skill routing baselines are conditioned on the top 50 retrieved skills and use some optimization method to improve performance. The exception is SkillDAG where it first retrieves top 5 skills then expands them until reaching top k. Its skills are ranked based on graph depth from top 5 retrieved skills and retrieval score. SkillSight is the default retriever. Qwen3.5-4B is the default model used in our experiments. The non-parametric baselines, including ours, do not output a continuous score like rerankers, so we rank them by the sum of their binary label and the retrieval score, where 1 is the label for selected skills and 0 otherwise. We use a temperature of 1 for proposing candidate predicates and 0 for evaluating predicates and downstream performance. RouterBSI is optimized for 10 iterations. In downstream tasks, we use k = 5 skills for SkillRouter and RouterBSItopk, and we set k = 10 for SkillDAG because k = 5 would match the retriever’s performance. 6 Results 6.1 Skill Routing Performance RouterBSI improves routing performance of base retrievers by an average nDCG@1 and nDCG@10 across retrievers of 67.0% and 39.7%, respectively Table 1.
