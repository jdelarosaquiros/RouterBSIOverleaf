Retention measures how much of that coverage remains when only the first k skills are used. For a method m and retriever r, let Recallm,r@k denote recall averaged over test tasks. The retention curves use Retm,r @k = 100 Recallm,r@k Recallm,r@50. This is a ratio of task-averaged recalls, rather than an average of task-level retention ratios. We evaluate k ∈{1, 5, 10, 25, 50}, and average the three retriever-specific retention values to obtain the curve in Figure 3. Each method is normalized by its own Recall@50. In particular, SkillDAG expands its initial retrieval set, so its normalization uses the expanded ranked pool. A method can have high retention but low retrieval coverage. Conversely, a method can recover more required skills at cutoff 50 but rank them too low for a small skill budget. We therefore report Recall@50 alongside retention, rather than interpreting the normalized curve alone as end-to-end recall. F.2 Results Across Routing Budgets Table 8 gives the numerical values of the mean retention curve, and Table 9 separates the retrievers. RouterBSI retains 90.5% of its cutoff-50 coverage at k = 5 and 94.6% at k = 10, averaged across retrievers. SkillRouter retains 83.9% and 87.9%, respectively. The difference is therefore visible at both small budgets, rather than only at the first position. SkillDAG illustrates the distinction between coverage and ordering. Its expansion increases Recall@50, but retaining only the first five skills discards a larger fraction of the recovered coverage. The observed recall values are nondecreasing with k for every method–retriever pair in the table.
