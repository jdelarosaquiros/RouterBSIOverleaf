The held-out gate evaluates changed skills against their original initialized predicates. In the single-predicate setting, its configured per-skill F1 score reduces to the fraction of correct binary judgments on validation tasks retrieving that skill, including true negatives. A changed skill is kept only on strict improvement over initialization; a tie or missing validation support causes rollback to the previous accepted predicate. This rule does not require improvement over the previous predicate’s validation score. Across the three both-enabled runs, 2,299 of 12,390 changed skill–iteration candidates are retained. The remaining candidates comprise 5,926 ties with initialization, 1,539 lower scores, and 2,626 skills without validation retrieval support. These are skill–iteration decisions, not distinct skills. Of the retained decisions, 262 score below the previous predicate while still improving over initialization. Thus, local update retention and aggregate ranking quality measure different outcomes.
