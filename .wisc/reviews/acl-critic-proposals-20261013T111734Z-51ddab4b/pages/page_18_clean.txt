D.3 Controlled Method Variants The replay-capacity settings use N ∈{1, 3, 5, 10} per relevance category while keeping the candidate count and length limit fixed. Retrieval-budget variants use 10, 25 or 50 candidates. The corresponding current-inclusive replay bound is 2N +1, not N total. Disabling the held-out gate retains the provisional candidate library without per-skill rollback. Disabling MBR changes selection to the first generated candidate satisfying the current-task checks; the proposal still receives retained positive and negative context. Thus this ablation removes replaybased candidate selection rather than all historical information from generation. Disabling both mechanisms combines those changes. The alternative replay-F1 objective computes RF1 = 1 − 2TP 2TP + FP + FN, with risk one when the denominator is zero. This is distinct from the default mean Boolean error and from the per-task validation predicate-pattern score. An accuracy-labelled validation variant coincides with the default validation score when every skill has exactly one predicate. E Extended Skill-Routing Results E.1 Ranking and Selection Metrics Let Lq = (s1, . . . , sK) be the ordered skill list for task q, and let Gq be its required skill set. Recall at cutoff k measures the fraction of required skills in the first k positions: Recall@ k(q) = |{s1, . . . , sk} ∩Gq| |Gq| . For nDCG, the relevance of each skill is binary. With yq,j = 1[sj ∈Gq], we compute DCG@ k(q) = k X j=1 yq,j log2(j + 1), nDCG@ k(q) = DCG@ k(q) Pmin(k,|Gq|) j=1 1/ log2(j + 1) . Thus, nDCG@1 is the fraction of tasks whose firstranked skill is required. Recall@1 can be lower when a task requires multiple skills, because it divides by the size of the required set. We distinguish the ranked list from the selected set Pq. Set-level F1 is F1(q) = 2|Pq ∩Gq| |Pq| + |Gq|. For strict predicate routing, Pq contains only skills whose predicates evaluate to true. A top-k routing list also contains predicate-negative skills when they fall within the ranked cutoff. The set-level F1 reported for predicate ablations therefore measures the selected set, rather than the first k entries of the complete ranked list. Dataset scores average the corresponding pertask metric. The overall score pools task instances across datasets; it is not an unweighted average of the six dataset scores. When comparing retrievers, we first compute this task-weighted score for each retriever and then average across BM25, Vector, and SkillSight. E.2 Results by Benchmark and Retriever Tables 5–7 expand the routing comparison into benchmark-level nDCG and recall. Each retriever group includes the base ranking, static selection, the routing baselines, and the initial and optimized RouterBSI predicates. All results use Qwen3.5-4B. The cutoffs of 5 and 10 expose whether a gain in the first-ranked skill also preserves more required skills within a small routing budget. The gains differ across benchmarks. For example, RouterBSI with SkillSight reaches nDCG@10 of 0.771 on BigCodeBench and 0.797 on ToolQA, compared with 0.616 and 0.390 for the base retriever. On TheoremQA, SkillRouter remains stronger in nDCG@10 for all three retrievers. MedCalc-Bench presents a different boundary: the Vector retriever already attains high coverage and ranking quality, leaving less room for improvement. These comparisons separate the effect of predicate refinement from the difficulty of the initial candidate pool.
