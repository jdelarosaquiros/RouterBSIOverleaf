\subsection{Impact of MBR based on Replay and Held-out Validation Gate}

Across three runs for each retriever in Figure~\ref{fig:mbr_replay_validation_std}, MBR with bounded replay alone achieved higher final performance on test dataset than the held-out validation gate alone: F1 increased from 0.440 to 0.527 and nDCG@10 from 0.683 to 0.736, relative improvements of 19.8\% and 7.8\%, respectively. Combining MBR with the held-out gate further improved final performance on unseen test tasks, increasing F1 by 3.47\% and nDCG@10 by 0.32\% compared with MBR alone. Disabling both only increases F1 and nDCG@10 by 11.4\% and 6.1\% which is much lower than the 81.8\% and 18.7\% increase when both are enabled. This supports empirical update selection without implying that unchecked updates invariably worsen performance. With both components enabled, diminishing returns appeared around iteration 5, by which point 96.8\% of the final F1 gain and 96.1\% of the final nDCG@10 gain had accrued. Later iterations added only 0.008 F1 and 0.005 nDCG@10, indicating an approximate plateau. This suggest diminishing opportunities for useful predicate refinement on the plateau: early iterations correct broad, frequent routing errors, while later iterations encounter harder or less common cases.

\subsection{Performance and Token Usage Tradeoff of MBR Replay and Held-out Gate}

Figure~\ref{fig:performance_token_usage} shows that combining replay and the gate provides the strongest final routing performance, while MBR alone reduces token usage by 10.8\% at the cost of a 3.4\% relative decrease in nDCG@10 compared with Both Enabled. Across configurations with and without the gate, enabling replay improves final routing performance by 11.6\% to 12.4\%, while increasing known run-local token usage by 40.6\% to 52.9\%.

\subsection{Skill Routing Performance across LLM Backbones}

The results show that cold-start performance of RouterBSI’s predicates created from skills without task feedback increases as the LLM backbone becomes more capable. For instance, the cold-start performance of Qwen 3.5 4B is 0.625 whereas Qwen 3.8 27B and GPT-OSS 120B performance is 0.716 and 0.753 (Figure~\ref{fig:routing_performance_across_llms_init}). The routing performance gains from RouterBSI optimization range from 28.0\% to 7.0\% decreasing with model size. The decrease in performance gained is likely cause by a plateau in routing performance between 0.800 and 0.844. Another notable result is that RouterBSI improved performance of the smaller Qwen3.5 4B enough to reach the performance of GPT-OSS,  a model with over 115b more parameters.
