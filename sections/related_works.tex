\subsection{Non-Parametric Routing Optimization}
% DOCX-P013-END

% DOCX-P014-BEGIN
Non-parametric routing optimization keeps the underlying model weights frozen while using feedback from task executions to persistently update an external state that affects subsequent routing decisions. \emph{Experience and memory updates} accumulate feedback into reusable metadata, memories, procedures, or workflows that influence subsequent routing, but they can undo previous improvements regressing performance \citep{wang2025agentworkflowmemory,wang2025inducing,xiao2025toolmemenhancingmultimodalagents,ouyang2026reasoningbank,pmlr-v306-mi26d}. \emph{Gated updates} limit regressions by discarding updates that regress performance based on surrogate objectives or rollback criteria, but these approaches do not directly minimize router error over seen tasks \citep{pmlr-v306-mi26d,shi2026programmatic}. JTPRO contains a criterion for selecting updates on sampled training minibatches and held-out validation performance, but it does not work in an online setting because it lacks a mechanism for bounding accumulated tasks \citep{ghoshal-etal-2026-jtpro}. \emph{Graph and other structural updates} improve routing by persistently modifying relationships among skills, such as dependencies, similarities, prerequisites, or hierarchical connections, allowing the routing structure itself to evolve \citep{zheng2025skillweaver,NEURIPS2025_136a45cd,zhang-etal-2025-toolexpnet,shi2026programmatic,bai2026skilldag}. However, expansion of skills with many connections can load many irrelevant skills significantly decreasing routing precision or even exceeding context limit. Overall, non-parametric approaches make routing adaptations explicit as natural language, but they require mechanisms for controlling regressions and management of accumulated experience. RouterBSI addresses these challenges by selecting candidate updates that minimize routing error over bounded replay of seen tasks. Our approach ensures non-increasing empirical error on the retained replay set while bounding replay evaluation cost.

\subsection{Parametric Routing Optimization}
% DOCX-P015-END

% DOCX-P016-BEGIN
\textbf{Parametric routing optimization improves future skill-selection decisions by updating learned model parameters from supervision, preferences, or execution feedback.}
% DOCX-P016-END

% DOCX-P017-BEGIN
Parametric routing optimization improves future skill-selection decisions by updating learned model parameters from supervision, preferences, or execution feedback. \emph{Learned retrievers and rerankers} train scoring functions to estimate relevance, compatibility, or expected utility between tasks and candidate skills, improving discrimination beyond fixed similarity measures but requiring labeled or feedback-derived training data \citep{qin2024toolllm,zheng2026skillrouter,wang2026skillisnotdocument,li2026skillsinjector,zhou2026mementoskills}. \emph{Trained direct invocation and generative selection} embeds the routing decision within a learned model that directly predicts, generates, or decides whether to invoke a capability, providing an end-to-end selection mechanism while keeping its applicability criteria implicit within model parameters \citep{ICLR2025_b646bdeb,qian-etal-2025-smart,chen2026skillskiplearningselective}. \emph{RL-trained sequential routing policies} optimize skill selection from downstream rewards over multi-step interaction, allowing routing behavior to adapt to execution outcomes but introducing additional training cost, reward-design requirements, and policy-update complexity \citep{wang2026sage,xia2026skillrl,zhang2026memskill,huang-etal-2026-toolomni,wu2026maestro}. Thus, parametric approaches can learn sophisticated routing behavior from experience, but they generally require large training data. Additionally, improvements are encoded in model weights making it difficult for humans to revise or inspect the learned routing criteria. Our approach uses natural-language applicability predicates which require much less data and are inspectable and editable by humans.

\subsection{Static Routing Without Feedback-driven Optimization}
% DOCX-P018-END

% DOCX-P019-BEGIN
Fixed routing approaches use predefined retrieval, organization, or inference-time decision mechanisms without optimizing the routing mechanism from task-specific feedback. \emph{Static similarity and case-based retrieval} selects candidates according to semantic similarity, lexical relevance, or similarity to previously observed cases; while efficient and simple, the matching criterion remains unchanged when routing errors occur \citep{wang2024voyager,guo2024dsagent}. \emph{Static hierarchical and structured retrieval} reduces the search space through predefined hierarchies, clusters, repositories, or graph structures, improving scalability but relying on structures that are not optimized from downstream selection failures \citep{li2026agentskillos,sun2026corpus2skill,wang2026graphskill,packer2023memgpt,liang2026skillnetcreateevaluateconnect,liu2025toolplanner}. \emph{Inference-time LLM composition and routing} delegates selection, composition, or orchestration to an LLM at inference time, allowing flexible task-dependent decisions but without converting those decisions or failures into persistent improvements to future routing \citep{chen2026cuaskill,xia2026grasp,NEURIPS2023_77c33e6a,NEURIPS2024_e41efb03}. Overall, these approaches can improve candidate organization and task-time routing, but routing failures are not corrected for future selections. Our approach adapts in black-box and online setting.
