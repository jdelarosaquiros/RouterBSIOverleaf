\subsection{Skill Routing Performance}
% DOCX-P060-END

% DOCX-P061-BEGIN
RouterBSI improves aggregate routing performance with both BM25 and SkillSight, achieving the highest dataset-averaged nDCG@10 and Hit@1. LLM Select is the strongest aggregate comparator, but RouterBSI increases mean nDCG@10 by approximately 16.7\% with BM25 and 16.5\% with SkillSight relative to it. SkillDAG provides smaller aggregate nDCG@10 improvements over retrieval alone, while Skill-Pro remains close to the retrieval baselines. RouterBSI achieves the best nDCG@10 on all six datasets with SkillSight and five of six with BM25, showing that its aggregate advantage extends across most datasets rather than depending on a single benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:skill_routing_performance}).
% DOCX-P061-END

% DOCX-P062-BEGIN
The exception is TheoremQA with BM25, where RouterBSI achieves 0.748 nDCG@10 versus 0.791 for LLM Select. Most failed cases of RouterBSI on TheoremQA were caused by failure to activate due to overly narrow predicates. RouterBSI could further improve its performance on these cases with more training iterations or relying on a human to directly edit the predicates.
% DOCX-P062-END

% DOCX-P063-BEGIN
\subsection{Downstream Performance}
% DOCX-P063-END

% DOCX-P064-BEGIN
Results demonstrate that combining RouterBSI labels with some additional skills as RouterBSI\_top5 has a 2.49\% to 5.59\% higher downstream performance compared to RouterBSI\_strict. RouterBSI improves average downstream task success over the no skills and LLM as selector baselines by average of 17.52\% and 6.62\%, respectively. RouterBSI\_top5's success rate is only 0.38 to 1.80 lower than the oracle which uses the ground-truth skills set. A notable result is SkilDAG's success rate exceeding oracle by an average of 4.75\%, which suggest expanding selected skills to include dependent skills improves downstream performance even if it doesn't improve routing performance (Table~\ref{tab:downstream_performance}).
